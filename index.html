Every weight comes with a reason

Most training apps hand you a number and leave you to trust it. When the number is wrong — and sometimes it will be — there is nothing to do but retype it, every set, forever.

Grasp shows the reasoning, and lets you disagree in one tap.

Leg Press 65 kg

Every set reached the top of your range — up 5 kg.

↑ 5 kg

Five kilograms because that is the smallest step the machine's stack actually has. A barbell moves in 2.5. A dumbbell rack moves in 2. The suggestion is always something you can physically load.

What it does

  • Plans around your gym

    Tell it what equipment you can reach and it will never suggest a movement you cannot do. Change gyms and today's session rebuilds itself.

  • Progresses on evidence

    Hit the top of your rep range on every set and the weight goes up. Miss it twice and it comes down. That is the whole rule, and you can state it back to us.

  • Works with no signal

    Everything lives on your phone. Gym basements, aeroplanes and dead zones change nothing, because there was never a server in the loop.

  • Tracks what you have trained

    A body map shades each muscle by how recently you worked it, so a week of training reads at a glance instead of needing to be added up.

  • Times your work and your rest

    Rest timers between sets, and interval timers for cardio — two minutes on, one minute off — with a sound at every change so you can put the phone down.

  • Lets you take your data

    Export everything as a spreadsheet or a complete backup, whenever you want, with or without an account.

Not an AI, on purpose

There is no language model deciding what you lift. Grasp's planning is ordinary, inspectable arithmetic: volume targets per muscle, a fatigue model based on what you logged, and a progression rule that fits in a sentence.

That is not a limitation we are apologising for. A system that guesses cannot tell you why it guessed, cannot be checked, and cannot run on a phone with no signal. This one can do all three.
