Privacy Policy

Last updated: 31 October 2026

Grasp is a workout planner and logger. This policy explains exactly what it stores, where, and what we can see. It is short because the app does very little with your data.

  • Grasp needs an account. Training is a record of your own body kept over years, and leaving it on a single device that can be lost is not looking after it — so signing in is what makes the backup, and the move to a new phone, possible.

    Your training is stored on your phone and backed up to your account. Nothing else is done with it: it is not sold, not shared, and not used to build a profile of you. Only the first sign-up needs signal — the planning and the weights are worked out on your phone, so every session after that works with no connection at all.

    The app has no advertising and no ad tracking. It records which screens and features you use, to make Grasp better, and you can switch that off. We do not sell or share your data with anyone.

    You can export everything and delete your account from inside the app, at any time.

    Friends and crews are your choice. If you add friends or join a crew, they see a few of your numbers (how often you train, your streak, your records and best lifts) — never your body measurements, never your workouts set by set. If you publish a program or comment, that is public.

    A coach only sees your training if you say yes. If you accept a coach's invite, they see your workouts, weights and records — never your photos, and your body weight only if you turn that on — and can send you a program. You can stop either any time.

Where your training lives

Everything — your workouts, sets, weights, routines, equipment, saved gyms and timers, and settings — is stored in a database file on your phone, and that copy is the one the app reads. It is what lets Grasp work with no signal.

That same data is backed up to your account so it can be restored if you lose the phone or move to a new one. That backup is the only copy that ever leaves the device.

Use Account → Export training to keep your own copy.

The one thing that happens before you sign in

When Grasp starts, it asks Expo's update servers whether a newer version of the app is available. This is how fixes reach you without waiting for a store release.

That request carries the app's version, your device platform (Android or iOS), and a random installation identifier generated on your device. Like any internet request, it also reveals your IP address to the server handling it.

It does not carry your training, your email, your name, or anything you have entered into the app. The identifier is random, is not linked to you, and is not used to build a profile or to track you between apps.

We mention it because "nothing leaves your phone" would otherwise be untrue, and a privacy policy that is nearly true is not worth reading.

If you create an account

We store:

  • Your email address, so you can sign in.
  • Your first name, if you typed one when signing up or if Google or Apple handed one over. It is used to greet you by it and for nothing else. Deleting your account deletes it along with everything else.
  • Your training: workouts, exercises, sets, weights, reps, the routines you build, your saved gyms and interval timers, and the settings that shape how workouts are generated.
  • Your phone's region setting, as a two-letter code such as SA or EG, the one you chose in your phone's language and region settings. It is not your location: no GPS and no internet address are used. It tells us roughly which countries Grasp is used in, and changes if you change it on your phone.
  • Two answers from setup, if you gave them: where you heard about Grasp, and why you track your training. Both are optional and both can be skipped.
    These are the only things Grasp stores that do not change how the app works for you. They exist so we know where people are finding us and what they are here for, which is how a free app with no adverts decides what to build next. They are not shared, not sold, and not used to target anything at you.
  • Anything you entered for the energy estimate, if you chose to: body weight, height, year of birth, and sex. All four are optional and can be left blank or cleared at any time — the app then either uses a rougher estimate or shows none at all. They are used for that calculation and nothing else.

We do not store your date of birth (the year alone is all the calculation uses), location, contacts, or health records from any other app. The only photographs we store are ones you choose to make public: a profile picture, or the cover of a program you publish (see below).

The data is held on servers operated by Supabase in Frankfurt, Germany. It is protected so that only your account can read it.

Signing in and syncing means your device connects to those servers, which necessarily see your IP address, as any server does when you connect to it.

Signing in with Google, Apple, or Facebook

You can sign in with an email address and password, or with your Google, Apple, or Facebook account. Which one you choose changes who checks that you are you — it does not change what Grasp stores.

When you use one of those, they confirm your identity and hand us your email address, and a first name if they have one. That is the whole of it. We do not receive your contacts, your friends, your photos, your posts, or anything else you do on their services, and we cannot see them. Grasp never posts anything anywhere on your behalf, and asks for no permission to.

Signing in this way means Google, Apple, or Facebook know that you have an account with Grasp, in the same way they know about any other app you sign in to with them. What you train is never sent to them.

Apple's Hide My Email gives us a relay address instead of your real one. Grasp works exactly the same way with it, and we have no way of telling the difference.

Apple sends your name once, the first time you authorise the app, and never again. Apple's own sheet lets you edit it, or replace it with anything you like, before you agree — whatever you put there is all we ever receive.

What we never collect

No advertising identifiers. No precise location: Grasp never asks for GPS or your location permission (the only place-related data is the approximate country described under "How the app is used", and your phone's region setting). No microphone, contacts, or calendar access. Grasp does not request these permissions.

The camera and your photos are asked for only when you choose to use them: to take a photo for a share card, to pick one, or to set a profile picture or a program cover. A share card is made on your phone and goes only where you send it; we never receive it.

We do not sell your data, share it with advertisers, or use it to train anything.

How the app is used

To see which parts of Grasp people use and where they get stuck, the app sends usage events to PostHog, an analytics service: the screens you open, and moments such as finishing the setup questions, starting or finishing a workout, applying a superset suggestion, sharing a card or connecting a health app.

Each event carries counts and choices only — how many minutes and sets a workout had, which split you picked. Never your weights, reps, body measurements, health data, name or email. There are no screen recordings and nothing you type is sent.

Once you are signed in, the events are linked to your account's internal ID, so your phone and tablet count as one person. PostHog also receives the app version, your device model, operating system and language, and works out an approximate country from your connection. The data is held by PostHog in the United States and used only to improve Grasp.

You can switch it off at any time under Account › Your data › Share usage data; nothing is sent from that moment. To have your usage history deleted, email grasp@alsouq.tech and we will remove it.

If the app crashes

When Grasp fails in a way it cannot recover from, it records what went wrong so it can be fixed: the error message and technical stack trace, which version of the app and which update were running, your platform, operating system version and device model, and when it happened.

It does not record what you were doing, what you had logged, or anything you typed. It is not a log of your activity — nothing is recorded while the app is working.

The report is stored against your account, in the same Supabase database in Frankfurt as your training. It is not sent to any third party. Grasp does not use Sentry, Crashlytics, or any other crash-reporting service, so nobody outside this project ever sees it.

If a crash happens with no connection, the report waits on your phone and is sent the next time there is one. Deleting your account deletes these along with everything else.

If you share a routine

Sharing a routine makes a link. So that the link works for a friend who does not have the app, the routine's plan is stored with it: its name, the exercises, sets, reps, rest and pairings, and the definition of any exercise you made yourself. No weights, no workouts, no name or email of yours. Anyone with the link can see that plan. The link belongs to your account, and deleting your account deletes it.

Your profile, friends and crews

Using Friends gives you a public profile: a username (made from your name, which you can change), the name you show, and a picture — one of Grasp's characters, or a photo you choose. Anyone can find you by username to send a friend request; nobody becomes your friend unless you accept, or open their invite link.

What a friend sees: your workouts this week and this month, your weekly streak, how many records you set lately, your workouts over the last eight weeks, the heaviest set on the main lifts you both do, when you last trained, and the program you follow. Never your body measurements, energy estimates, health data, or your workouts set by set.

Nudges and cheers are stored so the friend can see them, at most one of each a day. A crew is a group you joined through an invite: its members see each other's name, picture and number of workouts this month, and a short list of what happened. Joining a crew makes you friends with the person who made it, and you can leave at any time. The community challenge records that you joined it for the month; it shows only how many joined and finished, never who. Following someone who publishes programs is one-way: they see how many follow them, not who, and you see their published programs, not their training.

If you publish a program or comment

Publishing makes a program public: its name, the name you publish under, the cover photo if you add one, your description, and the plan itself (exercises, sets, reps). Anyone can see it, in the app and on its page on this website. How many people saved it, follow you or started it is shown to you as a count; nobody who started it is named. Comments are public too, with the name you comment under.

Before your first post you agree to the house rules: nothing abusive, hateful, sexual or spam, and real programs only. Words that break them are refused. Anyone can report a program, a comment or a person, and block a person; a blocked person no longer sees your programs or comments and cannot find you, add you or nudge you, and is not told. Content reported by several people is hidden automatically, and we review reports sent to grasp@alsouq.tech. You can unpublish a program, delete a comment, or delete your account, which removes everything you published.

If you connect health apps

Connecting is optional and off until you turn it on in Account → Connected apps. You can turn it off there at any time, and change exactly what Grasp may see in Apple Health or Health Connect.

Apple Health (iPhone) and Health Connect (Android). When connected, Grasp saves each workout you finish there (its start and end time, the type, strength training, and, if your profile allows an estimate, the energy), and reads, from whatever watch, ring, scale or app wrote them there: your body weight, heart rate, resting heart rate, heart rate variability, sleep, steps, active and total calories burned, and VO2 max.

What it reads is used on your phone only, and only for what you see in the app:

  • Heart rate: the average heart rate on a finished workout's summary.
  • Resting heart rate, heart rate variability and sleep: the Recovery, Sleep and Heart health cards in Insights → Body. For watches that keep no resting heart rate, heart rate during sleep stands in for it.
  • Steps and calories: today's steps and energy on the Training card in Insights → Body, next to your training week.
  • VO2 max: your fitness age and the VO2 max bar in Heart health, in Insights → Body.
  • Body weight: keeping your profile's weight current.

It is never sent to our servers, never used for advertising, and never shared. The one exception is your body weight: when a scale updates it, the new weight is saved to your profile and backed up like a weight you typed.

WHOOP. Linking WHOOP needs an account, because the link is kept with it. WHOOP gives us a key that lets Grasp ask, on your behalf, for your latest recovery, sleep and heart numbers. We store that key, and nothing else: when the app asks, our server fetches today's numbers from WHOOP and passes them straight back to your phone without keeping them. Turning WHOOP off, or deleting your account, deletes the key and tells WHOOP to end Grasp's access.

If you have a coach, or are one

Coach pages. Anyone who sets up a coach page makes public what they put on it: their picture, a line about them, what they coach, their social accounts, their programs, their own photos, and their clients' before-and-after photos. A coach may post a client's photos only with that client's consent, which the coach confirms in the app. If a coach adds a WhatsApp number, it is not shown on the page; it is handed to the person who taps “Get coached”, so WhatsApp can open a chat. A coach can ask for a verified tick, which we give by hand.

Questions to a coach. Anyone can ask a coach a question from the coach's page in the app or on the web (“Ask me”). Questions are anonymous unless a signed-in asker turns on “Show my name”, or a web asker types a name. The coach sees the question; only the ones the coach answers are shown, with the answer, on the coach's page for everyone. For a question asked on the web we keep a one-way hash of the internet address it came from, mixed with the coach, so we can limit how many questions one address sends and let the coach block it; the address itself is not kept. A signed-in asker is told in the app when their question is answered. The coach can remove any question.

Clients. A coach invites a client with a link. Nothing is shared until the client opens it and agrees. From then on, that coach — and only that coach — can see the client's workouts (when, which exercises, sets, reps and weights), records, and how many workouts they did each week. A coach sees the client's body weight only if the client turns on “Share my weight with my coach”; it is off until then, and turning it off, or ending the link, hides it again. To show it over time, we keep each change to your profile's body weight. A coach never sees a client's other body measurements, photos, health-app data or friends. A coach can send the client a program, which appears in the client's app and follows the coach's later edits. The client can stop it from their profile at any time, and the coach can end it too; once ended, the coach sees nothing more. One coach at a time.

Deleting your data

In the app: Account → Delete account. This permanently removes your account and everything backed up to it — including your profile and picture, friends, nudges, crews, follows, blocks, published programs and their covers, comments, your coach page and its photos, and any coach or client links. It cannot be undone.

Deleting your account does not delete the copy on your phone. To remove that, delete the app.

Keeping your own copy

Account → Export training gives you everything, as a spreadsheet (CSV) or a complete backup (JSON). It reads from the copy on your phone, so it works with no signal, and you can do it as often as you like.

Children

Grasp is not intended for children under 13, and we do not knowingly collect data from them.

Health and safety

Grasp suggests training weights based on what you have logged. It is not medical advice, and it cannot see your form, your health, or how you are feeling. Talk to a doctor before starting a new training program, particularly if you have an injury or health condition.

This website

This website (grasp.alsouq.tech) counts visits so we can see whether anyone reads it. The count is a daily tally and nothing else: which page was opened, in which language, roughly which country (worked out from your browser's time zone, not your IP address), whether the device is a phone, tablet or computer, which site linked here, and whether the App Store button was pressed.

It sets no cookies, stores nothing in your browser, and keeps no IP address, no browser details and no visitor id, so two visits from you are two anonymous counts that cannot be linked to each other or to you. Nothing is sent at all if your browser asks not to be tracked (Global Privacy Control or Do Not Track). The tallies are kept in the same EU-hosted database as the app's backups, and only we can see them. This counter is separate from the app and never touches your training data.

Pages for a published program or a crew invite read what is shown on them (the program, or the crew's name and progress) from our database; nothing about the visitor is stored.

Changes

If this policy changes in a way that affects what we collect, the app will say so before the change takes effect.
