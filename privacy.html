Privacy Policy

Last updated: 17 August 2026

Grasp is a workout planner and logger. This policy explains exactly what it stores, where, and what we can see. It is short because the app does very little with your data.

  • Grasp needs an account. Training is a record of your own body kept over years, and leaving it on a single device that can be lost is not looking after it — so signing in is what makes the backup, and the move to a new phone, possible.

    Your training is stored on your phone and backed up to your account. Nothing else is done with it: it is not sold, not shared, and not used to build a profile of you. Only the first sign-up needs signal — the planning and the weights are worked out on your phone, so every session after that works with no connection at all.

    There is no advertising, no analytics, and no tracking. We do not sell or share your data with anyone.

    You can export everything and delete your account from inside the app, at any time.

Where your training lives

Everything — your workouts, sets, weights, equipment and settings — is stored in a database file on your phone, and that copy is the one the app reads. It is what lets Grasp work with no signal.

That same data is backed up to your account so it can be restored if you lose the phone or move to a new one. That backup is the only copy that ever leaves the device.

Use Account → Export training to keep your own copy.

The one thing that happens before you sign in

When Grasp starts, it asks Expo's update servers whether a newer version of the app is available. This is how fixes reach you without waiting for a store release.

That request carries the app's version, your device platform (Android or iOS), and a random installation identifier generated on your device. Like any internet request, it also reveals your IP address to the server handling it.

It does not carry your training, your email, your name, or anything you have entered into the app. The identifier is random, is not linked to you, and is not used to build a profile or to track you between apps.

We mention it because "nothing leaves your phone" would otherwise be untrue, and a privacy policy that is nearly true is not worth reading.

If you create an account

We store:

  • Your email address, so you can sign in.
  • Your training: workouts, exercises, sets, weights, reps, and the settings that shape how workouts are generated.
  • Anything you entered for the energy estimate, if you chose to: body weight, height, year of birth, and sex. All four are optional and can be left blank or cleared at any time — the app then either uses a rougher estimate or shows none at all. They are used for that calculation and nothing else.

We do not store your name, your date of birth (the year alone is all the calculation uses), photographs, location, contacts, or health records from any other app.

The data is held on servers operated by Supabase in Frankfurt, Germany. It is protected so that only your account can read it.

Signing in and syncing means your device connects to those servers, which necessarily see your IP address, as any server does when you connect to it.

What we never collect

No advertising identifiers. No analytics or usage tracking. No crash reporting. No location. No microphone, camera, contacts, or calendar access. Grasp does not request these permissions.

We do not sell your data, share it with advertisers, or use it to train anything.

Deleting your data

In the app: Account → Delete account. This permanently removes your account and everything backed up to it. It cannot be undone.

Deleting your account does not delete the copy on your phone. To remove that, delete the app.

Keeping your own copy

Account → Export training gives you everything, as a spreadsheet (CSV) or a complete backup (JSON). It reads from the copy on your phone, so it works with no signal, and you can do it as often as you like.

Children

Grasp is not intended for children under 13, and we do not knowingly collect data from them.

Health and safety

Grasp suggests training weights based on what you have logged. It is not medical advice, and it cannot see your form, your health, or how you are feeling. Talk to a doctor before starting a new training program, particularly if you have an injury or health condition.

Changes

If this policy changes in a way that affects what we collect, the app will say so before the change takes effect.
